I run my hands over my skin to find bumps, and when I notice one, cannot stop thinking about it. And then I pick and pick and pick until I have a little wound. And then a scab and I pick and pick and pick.

I do this with worries, too. I sometimes do this thing I call a “probe”, to check if there’s something I should be worrying about (and then I obsess over it, catastrophize, avoid, probe to check if I’m still worried, repeat ad infinitum).

I know, with a lot of my thoughts, the best thing to do is briefly acknowledge them and then persistently redirect my thinking away, but it feels good, fundamentally, to wallow. 

I’ve been trying to find things to write about, and I can’t.

I’m not sure how much of that is AI, and how much of it is simply me spending all my time inside not doing anything but scrolling mindlessly on my phone.

Do I have interesting thoughts? Maybe. I was trying to write a piece earlier about birds, but I read my writing and really disliked it. I’ve wanted to express something about how certain birds embody certain “virtues” to me. Not in a religious or spiritual sort of way, but more as a source of comfort during the day. Maybe I should keep the essay focused on that.

I don’t know what I’m writing about. I don’t know if it is useful for me to be “writing” this or if it’s just a way of talking out my thoughts when there’s no one else around for me to talk at.

My feet look like a meth addict’s because I’ve been picking at them so much.

I’m trying to pull myself outward - out of my head, off my body, into the world around me. I guess I can start here, with the birds.

Some years ago my family was driving down California’s highway 1. We stopped at a vista point. I looked down and saw some birds, so far down, soaring above trees bordering the ocean. I don’t know what I felt, looking down at the birds, or even what species they were.

Goldfinches, at least, I know. 

Goldfinches embody joy. It’s their vivid yellow feathers. It’s their squeaky, babbling song. It’s their arc-up-soar-down flight. It’s a huge group of them clustered in a leafy tree, almost lost in the light green leaves, except for the occasional dart between branches.

Cardinals embody devotion. Male cardinals are attentive partners. Part of their courtship is feeding their mates. I saw a video once of a male cracking open a tough seed and feeding it to his partner. It looks like a kiss. He tilts his head back and forth when he checks on her. I cannot untie these birds from my husband, who cracks open seeds for me every day.

Robins embody hope. They stick with us through milder winters, and even if they depart in a tough Ithaca freeze, when they are back I know the sun will come back too. I cannot describe the thrill I feel when I hear their breathy, fluttering song for the first time that year. I love seeing them come out in droves after a rain. I don’t know why all this means they embody hope, but they do.

House finches don’t really embody anything specific to me, per se. But there’s a little couple that returns to our townhouse every year (at least, I will choose to believe they’re the same couple, and will steadfastly not google house finch life expectancy). Early in the spring, before they are busy rearing their young, the male sings NON STOP. My husband and I joke that it’s all he does (unemployed). He has such a distinctive song, always ending on the same trill. But I wonder sometimes if he’s not “unemployed”, but just workshopping his song. If every repetition, every slightly-off run at that same trill, is him trying to get closer to something he can’t say straight through. I mean, I do that too. I’m doing it right now, actually - circling the same few birds, the same few images, trying to get close enough to whatever it is I actually want to say.

I just remember once, when I was really down, when I was wondering what the hell the point of anything was, I saw Mr. House Finch’s little rosebud head and felt something. I don’t really know how to articulate what I felt.

What I can say is that, for at least a second there, I was outside my head, watching a bird.
