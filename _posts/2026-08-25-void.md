I came across this on a friend's blog a few days ago, at a point when I... I don't know, was procrastinating on a ton, ~~was~~ am so horrifically & incandescently mad at my PhD advisor, when I was feeling like the world, fundamentally, is not meant for someone as weak and stupid as me (especially someone who can't figure out her goddamn tenses). Reading how this person I very much look up to & admire had put this notion, and how clearly they'd articulated their struggles, filled me with a great sense of (guilty) relief, because even someone I aspire to be like has, is, struggling with this.

My version doesn't look exactly like theirs. I think the trigger for my O(voiding) is any task, however mundane, that makes me feel as though I must weigh my soul on the cosmic scales of worth (e.g. rejection of any sort, advisor-induced missed deadline, feedback, talking w academic siblings, job applications, infinitely many other things...). But the rest of it matched enough that I wanted to try their exercise on myself, because naming it straightforwardly feels like the first move toward long-term progress.

So: this person's twenty questions. Let's see if I have a Void Problem.

**Did you ever lose time from work or school due to the void?**
Christ, yeah. I lose entire weeks at a time to vegging out in bed, trying DESPERATELY to fill my mind with anything that isn't the task, anything that lets me forget I'm being a horrible, weak person for not doing it, and forget that the task is about to prove to me and everyone around me that I have no worth.

**Has the void ever made your home life unhappy?**
Yes. I withdraw... worst case, I get hostile. My husband picks up my slack around the house, spends hours comforting me when I'm hysterically sobbing. I completely ghost my friends when they need me. The people around me suffer for no reason.

**Did the void affect your reputation?**
Almost certainly. My relationship with my PhD advisor has been bad for a while, but my tendency to do this has, I fear, made him perceive my work ethic extraordinarily poorly.

**Have you ever felt remorse after voiding?**
Yes, and during. But the after is a "wiser" remorse, not the unchecked self-flagellation cycle that happens during.

**Did you ever void to dig yourself out of a hole of responsibilities?**
Yes.

**Has the void caused a decrease in your ambition or efficiency?**
Yes. It feeds a belief that there's nothing on earth I can be good at, or that anyone would pay me to do, because I'm weak, unwilling, lazy, and spend my time functionally drugging myself with distractions to stay out of my head.

**After a period in the void, did you feel you must return to the void as soon as possible to catch up on your duties?**
I don't think this applies to me. I bounce back and forth a ton. After voiding I become The Most Productive Person On Earth Who Has Everything Together, until something makes me feel bad again. Then, I guess, I keep tempting myself with the void, telling myself it'll make me feel better, until my willpower gives out.

**After completing a duty via the void, did you have a strong urge to enter the void again for another duty?**
Yes, because nothing else quiets my thoughts.

**Did you often void until the deadline had arrived?**
Yes. I give up after the fact, and don't tell anyone that I did, out of shame.

**Did you ever ask for favors from others to have more time to feed the void?**
Yes. The number of research meetings I've postponed because of this is... too many.

**Did you ever sacrifice enjoyable or desirable activities to the void?**
Yes. I withdraw from friends, my husband, my family, because I can't have them see me like this, and they can't keep me as safe from my thoughts as voiding can.

**Were you reluctant to stop voiding to do normal actions like eating, using the bathroom, or sleeping?**
I'm ashamed to say yes. I definitely put off sleeping regularly, and avoid the bathroom, drinking water, showering, because I can't have anything in front of me that turns my brain the fuck off and gets rid of my sticky thoughts. I need to not be in my head.

**Did the void make you careless of the welfare of yourself or your family?**
Yes, myself. It increases my suicidality and self-harm urges drastically, which in turn lowers my risk aversion.

**Did you ever void longer than you'd planned?**
Yes. I plan for one day of "just unwinding" and it stretches into a week.

**Have you ever voided to escape worry, trouble, boredom, loneliness, or loss?**
Worry is probably my #2 reason to void: the first, nebulously, is ~worth issues~.

**Have you ever committed or considered committing an illegal act as part of the void?**
Yes. (Side note: can anyone get me Adderall, or benzos? For legal reasons, I'm joking.)

**Did the void cause you to have trouble sleeping?**
Yes, I need to do anything but sleep, because if I sleep, the next day comes. And if I try to sleep, I'll be alone in my head.

**Do arguments, disappointments, or frustrations create within you an urge to void?**
Yes. Disappointments and frustrations most commonly. I hate feedback.

**Did you ever have an urge to celebrate good fortune by voiding?**
Yes.

**Have you ever considered self destruction or suicide as a result of the void?**
Yes.

My void feels like it cycles. Sometimes after a good self-esteem & no-bad-feedback-or-rejection-or-me-thinking-about-worth stretch I become The Most Productive Person On Earth Who Has Everything Together. For a while. And I'm super super driven and able to take on a ton. And I do this thing where I commit to stuff while feeling like this so that I have to commit when I would otherwise void (this never works while i'm voiding). And then something knocks that down again, like feedback, a bad meeting, disappointing myself, job applications, paper writing, fellowship applications, attending a conference, or a workshop, or a group lunch, or talking to my academic siblings, and I'm back in it. It's a big and very efficient shame-engine. It breaks itself, swings, and resets.

I (believe I) have a Void Problem. And I want to get better!

Maybe that's where this ends, for now. I want to get better. I like the idea of recovery. But I'm terrified to tell the person who first put this into words that I have this too. I haven't sent anything yet. I don't know if I will today. But I wrote this while voiding, and maybe that counts as the first move, even without the rest of it figured out. Yet.
